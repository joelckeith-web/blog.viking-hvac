# Ductless Mini-Splits in Phoenix: Cooling Garages, Additions and Hard-to-Cool Rooms

A ductless mini-split is a heat pump system that cools and heats a space without any ductwork. An outdoor unit connects to one or more indoor units through a refrigerant line, and each indoor unit conditions the room it sits in. In a Phoenix home, that makes a mini-split [a good fit](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling) for spaces where ductwork is hard to extend, such as a garage, an addition or a detached casita. It can also suit rooms with hot or cold spots.

A mini-split doesn't have to replace your [central air conditioning](https://viking-hvac.com/residential/air-conditioning/). It can [supplement an existing central system](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/ductless-mini-split-vs-central-heat-air) to manage the temperature in a specific room, such as an addition.

## What Is a Ductless Mini-Split?

A mini-split has two main parts: an indoor unit and an outdoor unit that [transfer heat through a connecting refrigerant line](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/what-is-a-mini-split). The indoor unit, often called a head, can be [mounted on an interior wall or ceiling](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling). A home can have one head or several, each connected to an outdoor unit. You may also see these systems called ductless heat pumps, which is the name ENERGY STAR uses alongside "mini split heat pumps."

No ducts are involved. Instead of pushing air through ducts in the attic, the indoor head sends conditioned air straight into the room where it's installed. That is why a mini-split can be [a good fit where extending ductwork is difficult](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling).

A ductless mini-split is also a heat pump, which means it moves heat from one place to another. In cooling mode, it [absorbs heat from inside your home and carries it through refrigerant lines to the outdoor unit](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/what-is-a-mini-split). In heating mode, the system works in reverse. The [outside unit extracts heat from the air, even when it's cold](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling), and the refrigerant carries that heat to the indoor head. In the Valley, that means one system can cool a space through the summer and warm it when winter mornings turn cool.

**Key Takeaway:** A mini-split is a heat pump with an outdoor unit, one or more indoor heads, and a refrigerant line between them. It cools in summer and heats in winter without any ductwork.

## Where a Mini-Split Fits in a Phoenix Home

Ductless systems can be a good fit where ducts are missing or hard to extend, and in rooms with hot or cold spots. The [ENERGY STAR list of good fits for ductless heat pumps](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling) includes several spaces you'll find around Valley homes:

- **Garages.** For most homes, the garage is a large space that gets treated as an afterthought. [Adding heating and air conditioning gives you more livable space](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/garage), whether you use it for woodworking, hobbies or new projects.
- **Additions and outbuildings.** Additions, sheds and garages are good candidates where extending ductwork or heating and cooling capacity is difficult. A detached casita is an outbuilding too, so it can be a good candidate when extending ductwork out to it is difficult.
- **Rooms that never feel right.** Mini-splits can help where hot or cold spots exist in a home, including rooms used as home offices.
- **Homes without ductwork.** Older homes with no existing ductwork that have never had central air conditioning are another fit.

A Valley garage in July shows why this matters. The heat builds up inside, and extending the home's ducts out to the garage can be difficult. Mitsubishi Electric offers garage systems in a [1:1 configuration](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/garage), which means one compact outdoor unit connected to one indoor unit.

Before you add equipment to a hard-to-cool room inside the main house, take a look at the ducts that serve it. [Rooms that are difficult to heat and cool](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/duct-sealing) can be a sign of poorly performing ducts. If that turns out to be the cause, our [ductwork services](https://viking-hvac.com/residential/ductwork-services/) may be the better first step.

**Key Takeaway:** Mini-splits can be a good fit where ducts are missing or hard to extend, such as garages, additions and detached casitas, and in rooms with hot or cold spots. If a room inside the house is hard to cool, check its ducts before adding equipment.

## Room-by-Room Control, and Its Limits

A mini-split gives you room-by-room control. A mini-split system [enables independent temperature control across individual rooms or spaces](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/what-is-a-mini-split). With more than one indoor unit, each area can have its own setting. That lets you keep a home office or garage workshop cooler without chilling the rest of the house.

A mini-split can also [supplement an existing central heating and air system](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/ductless-mini-split-vs-central-heat-air). It can manage the temperature in a specific room, such as an attic room or a home addition. In that setup, the central system keeps running the rest of the house, and the mini-split handles the room you added it for.

Each indoor unit does have limits on how far its air can reach. [Heat rises and cool air sinks](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling), and that affects which areas a single wall unit will help. Open spaces are generally easier to cool. It can be hard for heat to move through an open doorway, and heat can't move through a closed one at all. So plan on each head mainly serving the room it's in, especially when the doors are closed.

## What Does Installation Involve?

Good installation starts with sizing. Your contractor should verify the right size system with [a tool called Manual J](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling). Manual J is a load calculation. It works out how much heating and cooling your home needs to keep you warm in winter and cool in summer. It is [the national standard for sizing heating and cooling loads in homes](https://www.acca.org/standards/technical-manuals/manual-j). A proper load calculation is required by national building codes and most state and local jurisdictions.

Next comes the connection between the indoor and outdoor units. The line set is the bundle of small refrigerant pipes and wiring that runs between them. Because mini-splits [run on small pipes](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/what-is-a-mini-split), you don't have to rebuild walls or ceilings around ductwork. Careful routing helps too. [Running the line set through closets, attached garages, attics or stairways](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling) can cut down on patching and painting walls and ceilings.

The outdoor unit needs a good spot as well. When choosing where it goes, [keep these placement points in mind](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling):

- **Keep airflow open.** Avoid tight spots next to shrubs, walls or other structures.
- **Stay away from doors and windows.** Outdoor units release water when they defrost in winter.
- **Watch the roofline.** Check where roof runoff lands, and add a rain cap if needed.
- **Leave room for service.** A technician should be able to reach the unit easily.
- **Think about your yard.** Choose the spot that intrudes least on how you use your outdoor space.

**Key Takeaway:** A good mini-split installation starts with a Manual J load calculation. After that comes careful placement: indoor heads where their air can reach, a line set routed to limit wall repairs, and an outdoor unit with open airflow and easy service access.

## How Do You Keep a Mini-Split Running Well?

[Heat pump filters should be cleaned when they're visibly dirty or when the filter light comes on](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling). The indoor unit styles Mitsubishi Electric suggests for garages all have [washable filters](https://www.mitsubishicomfort.com/articles/garage).

Dust is part of life in the Valley. Because the cue for cleaning is how the filter looks, check it often instead of waiting for a set date.

Outside, keep the area around the outdoor unit open so [air can move freely](https://www.energystar.gov/products/ductless_heating_cooling). Trim plants back and don't stack storage against it.

**Key Takeaway:** Clean the indoor filters when they look dirty or the filter light comes on, and keep plants and storage clear of the outdoor unit so its airflow stays open.
