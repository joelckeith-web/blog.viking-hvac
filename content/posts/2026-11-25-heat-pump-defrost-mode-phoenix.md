# Why Your Heat Pump Steams and Goes Quiet on Cold Phoenix Mornings

A heat pump that goes quiet, whooshes and sends up a cloud of steam on a chilly morning is most likely running its defrost cycle. Defrost mode is a normal, automatic routine that melts frost off the outdoor coil so the system can keep heating your home. In most cases, you don't need to do anything at all.

It still helps to know what normal defrost looks like, because a few signs point to something else. At Viking Heating & Air Conditioning, a family-owned heating and cooling company in Chandler, we'd rather you understand your system than worry about it.

## What Is Heat Pump Defrost Mode?

A heat pump doesn't burn fuel to make heat. Instead, it moves heat. It [pulls heat from outside into your home in winter and out of your home in summer](https://www.energystar.gov/products/air_source_heat_pumps). If you're still comparing heating systems, our [complete residential heating guide for Phoenix Valley homeowners](https://blog.viking-hvac.com/blog/complete-residential-heating-guide-phoenix-valley-homeowners) walks through the main options.

To pull heat out of winter air, the heat pump's outdoor coil can get much colder than the air around it. The coil is the grid of thin metal fins inside the outdoor unit. When moist air passes over that cold coil, frost can form on it. A layer of frost [works like insulation](https://www.lennox.com/residential/lennox-life/consumer/heat-pump-defrost-cycle), which blocks the coil and makes the system work harder for less heat.

Defrost mode clears that frost and ice off the outdoor coil. An [automatic control reverses the system and stops the outdoor fan](https://resource.bakerdist.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/rheem_rqpm-a024jk000_article_1369225428406_en_uci.pdf) to defrost the coil when needed. For a short time, the heat pump switches from heating to cooling mode, which sends warm refrigerant to the outdoor coil. Once the frost has melted, the system goes back to heating your home.

**Key Takeaway:** Defrost mode is a built-in step, not a breakdown. Your heat pump briefly reverses itself to melt frost off the outdoor coil, then returns to heating.

## What Defrost Mode Looks and Sounds Like

During defrost, [the outdoor fan stops](https://resource.bakerdist.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/rheem_rqpm-a024jk000_article_1369225428406_en_uci.pdf), so the steady fan noise goes away for a short time. The unit may make some strange hissing or gurgling noises. You may also hear [a whooshing sound as the system shifts modes](https://www.lennox.com/residential/lennox-life/consumer/heat-pump-defrost-cycle). These sounds are normal.

Then comes the steam. Our technicians explain it this way: when a heat pump goes into defrost, it heats its frozen outdoor coil, and that is what causes the steam. The cloud may rise from the outdoor unit for a short time, and that is normal and harmless. The steam is water vapor.

You may also see water running out from under the unit. That water is melted frost. It must drain away from the unit to prevent damage from ice buildup.

How often defrost happens depends on your equipment. Some units start defrost on a timer. Others use an electronic control that senses coil and air temperatures to decide when a defrost cycle is needed.

**Key Takeaway:** A quiet outdoor fan, a whoosh, a short burst of steam and a little water under the unit are all normal signs of defrost. None of them on its own means your heat pump is failing.

## Why It Happens on Cold, Damp Phoenix Mornings

It's easy to assume a heat pump in the desert should never frost up. But the coil doesn't need freezing air to collect frost. While the refrigerant is pulling heat, the coil's surface can be much colder than the surrounding air. That's why [frost can form even when the weather feels mild](https://www.lennox.com/residential/lennox-life/consumer/heat-pump-defrost-cycle), and it [may start to form on the outdoor coil](https://resource.bakerdist.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/rheem_rqpm-a024jk000_article_1369225428406_en_uci.pdf) when the outdoor air turns cool but is still above freezing.

Phoenix winter mornings can get cool enough for that. The National Weather Service's [Phoenix New Year's climate page](https://www.weather.gov/psr/PHX_NewYears.html) lists a normal low of 45 degrees for January 1, based on 1991–2020 normals. That's above freezing, but it's [not unusual to see a heat pump defrosting in weather that mild](https://www.lennox.com/residential/lennox-life/consumer/heat-pump-defrost-cycle). So an occasional defrost cycle on a cold Valley morning shouldn't surprise you.

Moisture matters too. The outdoor coil can get cold enough for moisture in the air to freeze onto it, especially when temperatures hover around freezing and humidity levels are high. That's why you're more likely to notice defrost on a cold, damp morning, such as after a winter rain, than on a warm, dry afternoon. For other ways to get ready for the cooler months, see our guide on [how to prepare your HVAC system for colder weather in the Phoenix Valley](https://blog.viking-hvac.com/blog/prepare-hvac-colder-weather-phoenix-valley).

## Why Does the Air From Your Vents Feel Cooler?

You may notice [a slight drop in temperature during defrost](https://www.lennox.com/residential/lennox-life/consumer/heat-pump-defrost-cycle). Warm air production pauses briefly while the system melts frost outside. There are two reasons the air from your vents can feel cooler.

First, while the heat pump is melting frost outside, it isn't moving heat into your home in the usual way. To cover that gap, many heat pumps automatically switch on backup or auxiliary heat strips during defrost. Those strips keep the temperature inside from swinging.

Second, heat pump air feels different from furnace air even when everything is working. In heating mode, [the air from your supply registers is only a little warmer](https://resource.bakerdist.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/rheem_rqpm-a024jk000_article_1369225428406_en_uci.pdf) than the air already in your home. It will feel cool if it blows directly on you. That is normal heat pump behavior and doesn't mean defrost has gone wrong.

Sometimes your home keeps losing heat through the morning, or the air stays cool long after the steam has cleared. In that case, it's worth having the system checked.

## Steam or Smoke? How to Tell the Difference

Defrost steam follows a clear pattern. It shows up while the outdoor unit is defrosting, rises for a short time and fades once the frost has melted. It is water vapor, and water vapor has no burning smell.

Smoke doesn't fit that pattern. If what you see is dark, hangs around, smells like something burning, or keeps coming when the unit isn't defrosting, it isn't defrost steam. Don't wait for it to clear up on its own, and have a technician check the unit. If there is a fire, the [U.S. Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/home-fire-escape-plans/) says to get outside to your meeting place, stay there and call the fire department.

**Key Takeaway:** Defrost steam is short-lived, has no burning smell and only shows up during a defrost cycle. Anything dark or lingering, or anything that smells like burning, is not defrost and needs a technician.

## Signs Something Is Not Working Right

Defrost should start, finish and hand the job back to heating on its own. [Rheem's heat pump owner's manual](https://resource.bakerdist.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/rheem_rqpm-a024jk000_article_1369225428406_en_uci.pdf), for one, lists reasons to call a service technician, and several of them fit here. Watch for these signs:

- **Ice that keeps building.** The manual says a frost-covered coil is nothing to worry about "unless it continues to build up a thicker layer with areas of hard clear ice." Excess ice buildup is also on its list of reasons to call.
- **New screeching or grinding noises.** A whoosh during defrost is normal. The manual lists new, unusual noises as a reason to call a service technician.
- **Defrost that runs too often or too long.** If your heat pump [seems to enter the cycle too often or runs for unusually long periods](https://www.lennox.com/residential/lennox-life/consumer/heat-pump-defrost-cycle), that may point to a problem.
- **Short-cycling.** The manual lists a heat pump that is short-cycling (turning on and off rapidly) and not heating or cooling properly as another reason to call.

When defrost goes wrong, the cause can be in the parts that control it. Our [heating repair team](https://viking-hvac.com/residential/heating-repair/) works on heat pumps, and we can find out whether the problem is the defrost system or something else.

**Key Takeaway:** Frost that clears during defrost is normal. Ice that keeps building, new screeching or grinding, and defrost that seems stuck all call for a service visit.

## What You Can Safely Check Before You Call

Some heat pump problems have simple causes. [Rheem's owner's manual](https://resource.bakerdist.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/rheem_rqpm-a024jk000_article_1369225428406_en_uci.pdf) lists these checks a homeowner can make before calling for service:

- Make sure the power is on, and check the breakers or fuses for both the indoor and outdoor units.
- Look for anything restricting airflow, including blocked grilles and registers, dirty filters and a blocked outdoor coil.
- Confirm that your thermostat is set properly.
- Keep fences, shrubs and other obstructions at least two feet from all coil air inlets, and never use a weather cover over the outdoor unit.

Filters deserve a quick look. [Heat pumps work best with clean filters](https://www.energystar.gov/products/air_source_heat_pumps), and [dirty filters can restrict airflow](https://resource.bakerdist.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/rheem_rqpm-a024jk000_article_1369225428406_en_uci.pdf). The same manual says to keep the coil free of grass clippings, weeds and leaves. It also says to turn the electricity off before cleaning, and never to use the heat pump as a stand for garden hoses or tools.

Past those basic checks, we recommend leaving the coil, the wiring and the refrigerant parts to a licensed technician. A seasonal visit through our [preventative maintenance program](https://viking-hvac.com/residential/preventative-maintenance/) is a good time to have your heat pump looked over before the coldest mornings arrive.

**Key Takeaway:** You can check power, filters, thermostat settings and clearance around the outdoor unit yourself. Leave ice, wiring and refrigerant work to a professional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often does a heat pump go into defrost mode in Phoenix?

Frost is especially likely when temperatures hover around freezing and humidity levels are high. In the Valley, that means you are most likely to notice defrost on cold, damp winter mornings. If your unit seems to defrost too often or for unusually long periods, that may point to a problem.

### Can a heat pump frost over when it isn't freezing outside?
