## What This Extreme Heat Warning Means for Chandler

An Extreme Heat Warning is not a routine summer advisory. According to the [National Weather Service Phoenix](https://forecast.weather.gov/MapClick.php?lat=33.3062&lon=-111.8413), this level of alert is reserved for conditions that pose a significant risk to life, and it's issued when temperatures are expected to be dangerously high for an extended period. The current forecast shows highs of 111°F on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday, with only a slight cooldown to 110°F by Thursday and Friday — still well within dangerous territory.

For your home, this stretch of extreme heat means your air conditioning system will be operating at or near its maximum capacity for days on end. Compressors, condenser coils, and electrical components all work harder as outdoor temperatures climb, and there's very little overnight relief to allow the system to rest. Chandler's overnight lows during heat warnings often stay elevated as well, meaning your AC may run nearly continuously to maintain a safe indoor temperature.

This is exactly the kind of extended, high-demand period when marginal systems — ones with a failing capacitor, low refrigerant, or a clogged coil — tend to give out. If your system is already showing signs of strain, an Extreme Heat Warning is when small issues become full breakdowns.

> **Key Takeaway:** An Extreme Heat Warning means sustained, dangerous heat with little overnight relief. Your AC system will be under maximum stress from August 16 through at least August 21, making this the ideal window to catch small problems before they become emergencies.

## Why Pre-Storm AC Prep Matters More Than Ever This Week

There's a pattern that HVAC companies across the Phoenix East Valley see every summer: emergency calls spike dramatically during and immediately after extreme heat events. Systems that were "running fine" during milder weeks suddenly can't keep up once temperatures push past 110°F for multiple consecutive days. By the time a homeowner notices warm air coming from the vents, the household is already without cooling during the most dangerous heat of the year — and every other homeowner in Chandler, Gilbert, Mesa, and Tempe is calling for the same reason.

The smarter approach is proactive. A pre-heat inspection allows a technician to catch the issues that cause breakdowns before the warning period even begins:

- **Refrigerant levels** that are low due to a slow leak, which forces the compressor to work harder and can lead to complete failure under heavy load
- **Dirty or clogged condenser coils**, which reduce heat rejection and make the whole system less efficient exactly when efficiency matters most
- **Worn capacitors and contactors**, common failure points that often give out during periods of continuous high-demand operation
- **Airflow restrictions** from dirty filters or blocked ductwork, which cause systems to run longer than necessary to reach the thermostat setting
- **Thermostat calibration issues** that cause a system to run inefficiently or fail to accurately track indoor temperature

If you haven't had your system professionally checked yet this season, this is the week to do it — before the warning takes full effect. According to the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov), regular maintenance is one of the most effective ways to keep a cooling system operating close to its intended efficiency, particularly during periods of sustained high demand. That guidance is especially relevant this week, with three consecutive days forecast at 111°F.

## What to Inspect Before August 16

Whether you're scheduling a professional visit or doing a homeowner-level check, here's what deserves attention before the warning takes hold:

**Air filters.** A dirty filter restricts airflow, forces your system to run longer, and reduces overall cooling capacity. During a stretch of 110°F-plus days, a clean filter is one of the simplest ways to help your system perform closer to its rated efficiency. The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov) also notes that filter maintenance plays a role in indoor air quality, which matters when your home is sealed up tight against the heat.

**Outdoor unit clearance.** Walk outside and check that your condenser unit has clear airflow on all sides — no overgrown landscaping, stored items, or debris buildup. Restricted airflow around the outdoor unit reduces its ability to reject heat, which is critical when ambient temperatures are already pushing 111°F.

**Ductwork condition.** In Chandler's older neighborhoods and homes with attic-run ductwork, leaks and poor insulation can let cooled air escape before it ever reaches your living space — meaning your system runs longer to compensate. If you haven't had your ducts evaluated, our [ductwork services page](https://www.viking-hvac.com/services/ductwork-services) covers what a professional assessment includes.

**Thermostat performance.** If your thermostat is old, inaccurate, or non-programmable, it may be contributing to inefficient run cycles. Upgrading to a properly calibrated model can help your system respond more precisely during a week of sustained extreme heat. Learn more on our [thermostat installation page](https://www.viking-hvac.com/services/thermostat-installation).

**System age and history.** If your AC has needed repeated repairs over the past couple of summers, an extreme heat event is often the breaking point. Our [Repair or Replace Guide](https://www.viking-hvac.com/repair-or-replace-hvac-guide) can help you evaluate whether it makes more sense to invest in a repair now or plan for a replacement before next summer's heat arrives.

> **Key Takeaway:** Clean filters, clear outdoor unit airflow, sound ductwork, and an accurate thermostat are the four fastest checks you can do before an extreme heat event — and the four most common culprits behind mid-heat-wave breakdowns.
