## Why Sustained Extreme Heat Is Different From a Single Hot Day

A single 105°F afternoon is manageable for most properly maintained systems. But when temperatures stay in the 104–108°F range for four or five consecutive days, your AC doesn't get a break. Compressors run longer, cycle more frequently, and rarely get the downtime they'd normally have overnight when temperatures dip into the 80s.

This kind of sustained load exposes weaknesses that might not show up on a milder day:

- Marginal refrigerant charge levels that were "good enough" suddenly aren't
- Dirty coils that reduce heat transfer efficiency compound over multiple long run cycles
- Weak capacitors or contactors under continuous electrical stress are more likely to fail
- Restricted airflow from dirty filters or ducts forces the system to work harder for longer

The [Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov) notes that proper maintenance and airflow are essential to cooling system efficiency, particularly during periods of high demand. When a system is already compromised, days like the ones forecast this week are often when it finally gives out — usually at the worst possible time, like Tuesday's 108°F peak.

If your system has been running rough, making unusual noises, or struggling to keep up with the thermostat setting over the past few weeks, this is the week those issues are most likely to become full breakdowns. Homeowners in Chandler, Gilbert, and Mesa who've been putting off a service call should treat this forecast as the deadline, not next month.

**Key Takeaway:** Consecutive days above 104°F remove the recovery time your AC normally gets overnight, which is why marginal problems often become full system failures during extended heat events rather than isolated hot afternoons.

## Your Pre-Heat Wave AC Checklist

Before Tuesday's 108°F peak arrives, here's what Chandler homeowners should inspect or have inspected professionally.

### 1. Air Filter Condition
A clogged filter is one of the simplest — and most common — causes of AC strain. During heavy-use weeks like this one, check your filter now and replace it if it looks dirty or restricted. Restricted airflow forces your system to run longer to reach the same temperature, increasing wear during exactly the days you can least afford a breakdown.

### 2. Outdoor Condenser Unit Clearance
Walk outside and check your condenser unit. Clear away leaves, dirt, dust, or any debris blocking airflow around the coils. A restricted outdoor unit struggles to reject heat efficiently, which is especially problematic when ambient temperatures are already pushing 108°F.

### 3. Thermostat Function and Settings
Confirm your thermostat is responding accurately and holding temperature. If you've been considering an upgrade, a [smart thermostat installation](https://www.viking-hvac.com/services/thermostat-installation) can help your system run more efficiently during extreme heat by optimizing cycle timing and reducing unnecessary strain.

### 4. Refrigerant Levels and Electrical Components
This is where a professional inspection matters most. Low refrigerant charge and weakening electrical components like capacitors and contactors typically can't be diagnosed by a homeowner, but they're common failure points during sustained heat. A technician can catch these issues now, before Tuesday's peak, rather than after your system has already stopped cooling.

### 5. Ductwork Integrity
If certain rooms in your home have felt warmer than others recently, it could point to duct leaks or damage reducing delivered airflow. [Ductwork services](https://www.viking-hvac.com/services/ductwork-services) can identify and seal leaks, which matters even more when your system is working overtime during a heat event like this one.

**Key Takeaway:** A pre-heat wave inspection should include filter condition, outdoor unit clearance, thermostat accuracy, refrigerant levels, electrical components, and ductwork integrity — the areas most likely to fail under sustained 100°F-plus demand.
