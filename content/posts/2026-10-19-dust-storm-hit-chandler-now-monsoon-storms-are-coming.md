## What to Check on Your HVAC System Right Now

Before the next round of storms rolls in, take a few minutes to assess your system's condition. You don't need to be an HVAC technician to spot warning signs:

- **Look at your outdoor unit.** Check for visible dust, dirt, or debris accumulated around the base, fins, and coils. A light film of dust is normal; caked-on buildup is not.
- **Listen for unusual sounds.** Grinding, rattling, or humming that wasn't there before the dust storm could indicate debris has worked into the fan motor or blower assembly.
- **Monitor airflow.** If vents inside your home feel weaker than usual, dust-clogged coils or a dirty filter may be restricting airflow.
- **Check your air filter.** Dust events can load up filters faster than normal. A clogged filter forces your system to work harder and can degrade indoor air quality.
- **Test your thermostat.** If you experienced any power flickers during the wind event, make sure your thermostat settings and programming are still intact.

If anything seems off, don't wait. Minor airflow restrictions and dust intrusion are far easier — and less costly — to resolve now than after they've caused compressor strain or electrical damage. Homeowners unsure whether a repair or a full system evaluation is needed can reference our [Repair or Replace HVAC Guide](https://www.viking-hvac.com/repair-or-replace-hvac-guide) for guidance on next steps.

For homes relying on evaporative cooling, dust storms pose an additional concern. Blowing dust can clog pads and water distribution systems, reducing cooling efficiency right when you need it most. If your swamp cooler has been running sluggish since the storm, our [swamp cooler services](https://www.viking-hvac.com/swamp-cooler-services) team can diagnose the issue.

**Key Takeaway:** A quick visual and audio check of your outdoor unit, filter, and thermostat can reveal dust-related damage before it becomes a costly repair. When in doubt, schedule a professional inspection.
